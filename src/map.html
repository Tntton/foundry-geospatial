Methodology

How we score Australian primary care markets

Methodology is organised into two layers: regional-level scores (SA3 geographic units) and clinic-level metrics (individual practice locations). All are transparent by design, fully reproducible from public sources, and open to adjustment. This page documents each score's sub-drivers, word equations, and default parameterisation.

Part 1: Regional Analysis (SA3 Level)

Three synthetic scores characterise every SA3 region: the Composite Score, Workforce Risk Score, and Whitespace Score. Each is fully adjustable via the map interface to stress-test alternate investment theses.

Composite Score 0 – 100

The headline investment-attractiveness score. Each SA3 receives four sub-scores (each independently normalised to 0–100), then combined into a weighted average. Higher is more attractive as a consolidation target. Default weights reflect Foundry's base-case methodology; adjust them in the map rail to stress-test alternate theses.

Word equation
Composite = (30% × Demand) + (35% × Supply) + (20% × Competition) + (15% × Economics)
01
Demand
Default contribution · 30%
Captures the volume and trajectory of healthcare demand. Regions with more people, faster population growth, and a higher share of seniors (who consume GP services at roughly 2× the average rate) score higher.
Demand score ∝ Population2025 + Annual growth rate + % aged 65+
Population (2025) — ABS estimated residents, SA3 level Annual growth rate — population CAGR over trailing 5 years % aged 65+ — share of population in highest-utilisation cohort
All three sub-drivers are positively weighted — higher values push the score up.
02
Supply
Default contribution · 35%
Measures existing GP clinic capacity relative to population size. Regions with fewer clinics per capita score higher — thin supply signals undersupply, lower competitive intensity, and greater pricing power for a consolidating operator.
Supply score ∝ 1 ÷ (Clinics per 10,000 residents)
Clinic count — total GP practice locations within SA3 boundary Clinics per 10,000 residents — normalised density; lower → higher score
Inversely weighted — fewer clinics per capita produces a higher Supply score.
03
Competition
Default contribution · 20%
Indexes the consolidation runway available. Markets dominated by independent practices offer more fragmented ownership and greater room to consolidate; markets already heavily concentrated by corporate operators have less runway for an incoming platform.
Competition score ∝ % Independent practices
% Independent — share of clinics not affiliated with a recognised corporate group (positively weighted) % Corporate — share operated by named groups e.g. ForHealth, Healius, IPN (negatively weighted) % Public / Non-profit — out-of-scope for private consolidation; not penalised but not rewarded
Higher independent share → higher Competition score.
04
Economics
Default contribution · 15%
Proxies the unit economics of running a practice in each region. Higher incomes support private billing and mixed billing revenue; the MMM classification incorporates rurality-linked cost and staffing dynamics.
Economics score ∝ Median household income + MMM-adjusted attractiveness
Median household income — ABS 2023 survey; higher income → higher billings potential MMM classification — Modified Monash Model class 1–7; lower (metro) classes treated as more economically attractive for consolidation
Positively weighted — wealthier, more accessible regions score higher on Economics.

Tier assignment

Composite scores are bucketed into five absolute tiers. Tiers hold their meaning when weights are adjusted — a Tier 2 region under one thesis is directly comparable to a Tier 2 region under another.

Tier 185–100Exceptional — top-decile market, strong consolidation thesis
Tier 270–84Strong — high-conviction region with multiple targets
Tier 355–69Moderate — selectively attractive; thesis-dependent
Tier 440–54Weak — below-median fundamentals; deprioritise unless distressed
Tier 50–39Poor — low priority under base-case methodology

Workforce Risk Score 0 – 100

A supply-side stress indicator. Higher scores flag SA3s where GP workforce constraints are most acute — either because the region is GP-sparse, has an ageing GP population approaching retirement, sits inside a government-designated area of GP need, or all three. The score is independent of the Composite and can be weighted separately.

Word equation
Workforce Risk = (40% × Supply density risk) + (30% × Age cohort risk) + (30% × DPA score)
A
Supply density risk
Default contribution · 40%
Lower GP FTE per capita → higher risk. The SA3's dominant MMM class (from F-06) is used to look up the national HWD benchmark for that remoteness class. Normalised across the full 7-class MMM range so that the most GP-sparse class (MM6, 6.76 FTE/10k) scores 100 and the most GP-dense (MM3, 13.47 FTE/10k) scores 0.
Supply risk = (FTEmax − FTEMMM class) ÷ (FTEmax − FTEmin) × 100
GP FTE per 10,000 pop. — HWD 2025, imputed from SA3's MMM_Dominant class. Not an SA3-specific count — a class-level benchmark. MMM range (2025) — Min 6.76 (MM6 · Remote) → Max 13.47 (MM3 · Large rural). Note: MM3 outperforms MM1 metro (11.21) due to rural health workforce policy incentives.
B
Age cohort risk
Default contribution · 30%
A higher share of GPs aged 55+ signals near-term retirement pressure and pipeline vulnerability. Same MMM-class imputation as above. Normalised so the class with the highest % ≥55 (MM1 metro at 41.8%) scores 100 and the lowest (MM2 regional cities at 26.6%) scores 0.
Age risk = (Pct55MMM class − Pct55min) ÷ (Pct55max − Pct55min) × 100
% GP workforce aged ≥55 — HWD 2025 (55–64 GPFTE + 65+ GPFTE) ÷ total GPFTE, by MMM class Class range (2025) — Min 26.6% (MM2) → Max 41.8% (MM1). Metro areas carry the highest retirement risk cohort nationally.
C
DPA flag score
Default contribution · 30%
A binary step function. If an SA3's boundary intersects a government-declared Designated Provider of Area polygon, it is flagged under one or both DPA programs. Each program reflects a distinct government finding that the area is underserved by GPs.
DPA score = 100 if both flags · 50 if one flag · 0 if neither
DPA Bonded — area designated under the Bonded Medical Program. Doctors on bonded scholarships must serve here; indicates chronic underservice. DPA GP/IMG — area designated under the s19AB restriction (General Practice / International Medical Graduates). IMGs can only access MBS in these areas; indicates persistent workforce gaps. Source — DoctorConnect catchment shapefiles, 2020 vintage, EPSG:4283. Spatial intersection at SA3 polygon level.
Worked example — Alice Springs (MMM 6)
FTE/10k = 6.76 → Supply risk = (13.47 − 6.76) ÷ (13.47 − 6.76) × 100 = 100
% ≥55 = 33.8% → Age risk = (33.8 − 26.6) ÷ (41.8 − 26.6) × 100 = 47
Both DPA flags → DPA score = 100
Workforce Risk = 0.40 × 100 + 0.30 × 47 + 0.30 × 100 = 84

Whitespace Score 0 – 100

A binary-signal composite that identifies "whitespace" markets — regions where multiple structural tailwinds align for a new entrant or consolidator. Unlike the Composite Score, which rewards high attractiveness, the Whitespace Score specifically rewards regions where the incumbent landscape is weak and the case for entry is structural rather than financial. Each of four independent binary signals contributes 25 points.

Word equation
Whitespace = 25 × [Economics ≤ 50] + 25 × [Corporate share < 30%] + 25 × [Demand ≥ 60] + 25 × [Workforce Risk ≥ 60]
+25
Low economic attractiveness
Economics Score ≤ 50. Markets with below-median household incomes and remote MMM classifications are less attractive to corporates deploying capital under a private-billing thesis. This leaves a structural gap that a mission-aligned or bulk-billing operator can exploit.
Signal fires when: Economics Score ≤ 50
+25
Low corporate penetration
Corporate share < 30%. The market has not yet been consolidated by large GP operators. A low corporate share suggests the region has been passed over or is in the earliest stages of roll-up — both are entry opportunities.
Signal fires when: Corporate share < 30% of total clinics
+25
High demand pressure
Demand Score ≥ 60. Significant population size, growth trajectory, or senior cohort ensures that a new entrant enters into a market with structural demand — not one that requires demand creation.
Signal fires when: Demand Score ≥ 60
+25
Elevated workforce stress
Workforce Risk Score ≥ 60. GP retirement pressure and/or DPA designation create service gaps that require new supply. An entrant filling this gap may benefit from government incentive programs (bulk billing incentives, rural loading, locum support) unavailable in lower-risk markets.
Signal fires when: Workforce Risk Score ≥ 60
100All 4 signalsVery high whitespace — all structural tailwinds present simultaneously
753 of 4 signalsHigh whitespace — strong structural case for entry
502 of 4 signalsMedium whitespace — mixed signals; thesis-dependent
251 of 4 signalsLow whitespace — isolated signal only
00 signalsNo whitespace — market does not exhibit structural entry tailwinds

GP Billing Metrics SA3 level

The five GP Billings map views surface Medicare Non-Referred Attendances (GP consults billed under MBS) at the SA3 level, using a rolling 4-quarter window ending Dec 2025. These are market-sizing and billing-mix metrics — not synthetic scores. They describe how much revenue is flowing through each SA3's GP sector, how price-sensitive patients are (bulk-billing %), and how fast both are growing.

Coverage: 335 of 336 SA3s have NRA data. Only Lord Howe Island is excluded (MBS statistically suppressed due to very low service volumes).

Fees per Service = Total Fee Charged ($) ÷ Total Service Count
Bulk Billing % = Bulk-Billed Services ÷ Total Services × 100
3-Year Fee CAGR = (Rolling 4Q Fees ÷ Same 4Q 3 years prior)1/3 − 1
3-Year BB% CAGR = (Current BB% ÷ Prior BB%)1/3 − 1
Fees/Svc
Avg fees per service
Total MBS fee charged in the SA3 divided by total service count. A proxy for the billing intensity and patient-mix of the market. Higher fees reflect more complex consults, longer items, and/or lower bulk-billing discount. Percentile-ranked 0–100 across SA3s for map display.
Source: Fee Charged ($) ÷ Services (MBS NRA)
Total $
Total fees
Gross fees charged to all GP NRA services in the SA3 over the rolling 4-quarter period. The total fee pool — irrespective of what Medicare pays vs. patient pays — is the best proxy for market revenue size. Percentile-ranked 0–100 for map display.
Source: Fee Charged ($) rolling 4Q (MBS NRA)
BB%
Bulk billing rate
Proportion of consults bulk-billed (patient pays nothing). High BB% markets (remote, low-income) are characterised by thin margins but high volume and government supports. Low BB% markets (affluent metro) generate private billing revenue. The map uses a diverging colour scale centred at 50%.
Source: Bulk-Billed Services ÷ Total Services (MBS NRA)
CAGR
3-year growth rates
Compares rolling 4 quarters ending Dec 2025 vs. same 4 quarters ending Dec 2022. Positive fee CAGR indicates market revenue expansion. Positive BB% CAGR indicates mix shift toward bulk-billing. Both use a diverging red-neutral-green scale centred at 0%.
Baseline: rolling 4Q ending Dec 2022 (same quarter 3 years prior)
NRA limitations. NRA covers GP Non-Referred Attendances only. It excludes allied health, specialist, pathology, diagnostic imaging, and after-hours urgency items. Telehealth and service-type breakdowns (item-level) are not available in this extract — a future upgrade could layer MBS item-level quarterly data (DS-07) for service-mix analysis. Metrics are SA3 aggregates; within-SA3 provider variation is not captured.

Part 2: Clinic-Level Analysis

Individual clinics are profiled across three dimensions: ownership classification, archetype (format & billing model), and catchment. Each clinic location generates its own metrics independent of regional aggregates.

Ownership classification

Every clinic is tagged across three ownership categories: Corporate (operating under a recognisable corporate brand), Independent (all others), and Public / NGO (government or not-for-profit). Any clinic type may be an acquisition target — corporate operators can be acquired as part of a platform or network transaction, and independents may be available for individual site roll-up.

Important caveat — read before quoting ownership figures. Ownership classification is Foundry's own internal tagging, applied against the clinic location dataset. Clinics operating under a recognisable corporate brand (e.g. ForHealth, IPN, Sonic, Healius, Tristar) are flagged 'Corporate'; all others default to 'Independent'. In practice, many corporate operators run sites under unbranded or locally-named entities — particularly in regional markets and post-acquisition portfolios — and these default to the 'Independent' bucket.

Treat ownership classification as a directional screening signal, not an underwriting input. For any region under serious consideration, manual ownership verification (ASIC, ABN lookup, GP-level interviews) is required before deployment of capital. The model is a screening tool — not a deal-ready target list.

Archetype Classification (Format, Billing, Ownership)

Every clinic in the map is classified across three dimensions — Format (physical scale), Billing Model (payment mix), and Ownership (corporate affiliation). These classifications enable market segmentation, competitive benchmarking, and targeted acquisition strategy. All three use a three-tier fallback system: website signals (high confidence) → name keywords (medium confidence) → SA3-level aggregates (low confidence).

1
Format
Physical scale of the clinic — number of GPs, rooms, and operational footprint. Larger clinics may have higher leverage potential, faster onboarding, and stronger bulk-billing negotiations. Smaller clinics may have higher per-clinic margins but require more roll-up volume for consolidation economics.
Classification priority: Doctor count (website scrape) → Name keywords → NHSD service type
Big-box — ≥6 GPs OR website signals "multi-clinic", "chain", "medical centre". High confidence: doctor count from website scrape. Mid-format — 3–5 GPs OR name contains "medical centre", "health hub", "group practice". Medium confidence: name keyword match. Small — 1–2 GPs OR name contains "family clinic", "family doctor", "neighbourhood". High confidence: doctor count from scrape. Unclassified — Insufficient signal across all tiers. Treated as independent variable in analysis; distribution inferred from SA3-level clusters.
Distribution: 14.1% Big-box · 25.1% Mid-format · 27.9% Small · 32.9% Unclassified.
2
Billing Model
Revenue mix between bulk-billed (100% patient subsidy via Medicare) and private-billed (patient pays gap fee). Bulk-billing dominated markets tend toward lower per-service margins but higher volume and government support. Private-billing markets extract higher per-service revenue but face price elasticity risk. Mixed billing clinics hedge both channels.
Classification priority: Website billing keywords → SA3-level bulk-billing %, no fallback
Bulk billing — Website text contains "bulk billing", "100% bulk", "no gap", "gap-free" (high signal weight: +2 per phrase match). Indicates policy commitment to 100% subsidy. Private — Website text contains "fee-for-service", "private fees", "gap fees" (high signal: +2). Indicates mixed or fully private billing model. Mixed — Signals for both bulk and private present, or ambiguous phrasing. Reflects real-world practice: many clinics do both. Unclassified — No website signals detected. Fallback to SA3-level bulk-billing % from MBS NRA: >70% BB% → assume Bulk; <30% BB% → assume Private; 30–70% → assume Mixed.
Distribution: 15.4% Bulk · 4.7% Mixed · 4.6% Private · 75.3% Unclassified (mostly uses SA3 fallback).
3
Ownership
Corporate affiliation, reconciled from two sources. Website scraping detects current corporate signals; original NHSD registry contains historical corporate brands and Public/NGO designation. The two sources are merged intelligently to maximise accuracy: website-detected corporates are trusted (current); known corporates missed by scraping are recovered from the registry; Public/NGO is always preserved.
Ownership_Final = [Original=Public/NGO] OR [Enriched=Corporate] OR [Original=Corporate, Enriched=Independent] OR Enriched=Independent
Corporate — Website contains recognized corporate brand OR website scraping detected ownership signals (e.g. "Sonic", "IPN", "MyHealth") OR known corporate chain name. Known brands: ForHealth, Healius, Sonic, IPN, Eastbrooke, Our Medical, Ochre Health, SmartClinics, Atticus, Next Practice, Medicross, TLC Primary Care, Qualitas Health, Medical One, Tristar, Affiliated Doctors, Pulse, ProMedico, Doctors Lounge, Healthpoint. High confidence when website signals present. Independent — Clinic operates without recognisable corporate affiliation. Includes sole proprietors, partnerships, and small unbranded groups. Default classification when no corporate signals detected. Public / NGO — Government-funded community health, Aboriginal health services, or registered not-for-profit. Source: NHSD_SERVICE_TYPE field (e.g. "Maternal, child and family health service", "Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander health service"). Out of scope for private consolidation.
Distribution: 10.9% Corporate · 81.1% Independent · 8.0% Public / NGO. Reconciliation recovered 300 clinics missed by enrichment and preserved 633 Public/NGO clinics that enrichment did not classify.
Worked example — "Moonee Ponds Medical Clinic" (Vic)
Format: Website scrape found 7 doctor names → Big-box (high confidence)
Billing: Website text: "bulk billing", "gap-free" → Bulk billing (high confidence)
Ownership: Original registry: Independent. Website scrape: detected corporate signals → Corporate (high confidence). Final: Corporate (website-based, current)
Archetype limitations. Format classification is incomplete (32.9% unclassified) pending ancillary services scrape (physiotherapy, pharmacy, dental presence) which will upgrade "medical centre" clinics from mid-format to big-box once room count is confirmed. Billing model relies heavily on SA3-level fallback (75.3% unclassified at clinic level) — individual clinic billing mix may vary significantly within the region. Ownership classification combines website-current signals with historical registry data; manual verification (ASIC, ABN) recommended before acquisition targeting.
Classification tiers (in priority order)
Tier 1 (high confidence): Website scrape signals (keyword density, page content), clinic name patterns (e.g., "medical centre"), or direct doctor count from web scrape
Tier 2 (medium confidence): NHSD clinic name keywords, service type descriptors
Tier 3 (low confidence): SA3 regional aggregate % (e.g., bulk billing %, corporate penetration rate)
01
Format (clinic size)
Three size classes based on GP headcount and practice structure.
Big-box: ≥6 GPs or website signals multi-location / corporate scale
Mid-format: 3–5 GPs or keywords like "medical centre", "health hub", "group practice"
Small: 1–2 GPs or keywords like "family clinic", "family doctor", "family practice"
GP headcount (Tier 1): Extracted from /doctors, /team, /our-doctors pages via regex; source: website scrape Clinic name keywords (Tier 2): Pattern match on ORGANISATION_NAME field Fallback (Tier 3): Not applicable; clinics without Tier 1/2 signals are marked 'Unclassified'
Higher GP count → Big-box. Clinic name signals are scored; ambiguous names default to Unclassified.
02
Billing Model (reimbursement)
Three billing strategies based on revenue reimbursement mix.
Bulk: Predominantly bulk-billing (Medicare rebate only); website states "bulk billing" or "100% bulk billing"
Mixed: Clinic accepts both bulk and private patients; website mentions both billing types
Private: Predominantly private-billing (patient gap payment); website emphasizes private consultation fees
Billing keywords (Tier 1): Keyword extraction from clinic website homepage and contact pages (bulk_billing, private, mixed) SA3 regional fallback (Tier 3): If website scrape yields no billing signals, use SA3's bulk billing % from Medicare quarterly statistics. >70% BB in region → assume 'Bulk'; <30% → assume 'Private'; else 'Mixed'
Explicit website signals preferred. SA3-level fallback used only when no clinic-level data is available.
03
Ownership (operator type)
Three ownership classes based on corporate affiliation.
Corporate: Clinic is part of a named corporate group (e.g., ForHealth, Healius, IPN, My Health)
Independent: Single-location or locally-owned practice with no corporate affiliation
Public / NGO: Government-run, community health service, or non-profit operator
Corporate brand detection (Tier 1): Website scrape identifies corporate marketing language or logo. Fallback to NHSD clinic name pattern match against known corporate brand list (~50 major groups). Known corporate list (Tier 2): Curated list including: Healius, My Health, Sonic HealthPlus, IPN, East Brooke, ForHealth, Primary Care Australia, Medicross, Ochre Health, SmartClinics, Family Doctor Network, and others. Reconciled from website data and NHSD registry. NHSD service type (Tier 3): If clinic name contains "Ltd", "Corporation", "Pty Ltd", infer Corporate; else default to Independent
Website corporate signals > name-based keywords > NHSD fallback. Public/NGO is preserved from NHSD classification and never overridden by web signals.

GP FTE Estimation — Effective Capacity Model

This model estimates GP FTE (Full-Time Equivalent) based on the assumption that GPs typically work part-time or split their time across multiple locations.

Core formula
GP FTE = GP Headcount × 0.75

This uniform factor of 0.75 is applied to all clinics (both corporate and independent) and reflects industry data suggesting that GPs commonly work part-time, flexible schedules, or across multiple locations.

Example: A clinic with 4 identified GPs → FTE = 4 × 0.75 = 3.0
Known limitation Implication
Uniform factor The 0.75 multiplier is applied uniformly across all clinics. Actual FTE may vary by clinic size, ownership, and location — this metric provides a conservative lower-bound estimate.
Incomplete GP data Not all clinics have complete GP headcount data. Metrics marked with > indicate a lower bound; percentage shows what proportion of clinics in the catchment have confirmed GP counts.
Part-time vs full-time distinction The model does not distinguish between GPs employed part-time at a single location versus those working across multiple locations. The 0.75 factor is an average across both patterns.
Terminology
GP FTE represents the estimated full-time equivalent based on headcount. It is a conservative estimate and should be used for high-level capacity planning, not detailed workforce allocation.

Lower-bound notation (>) is used when displaying metrics based on incomplete data — it indicates that the true value may be higher.

Catchment Analysis (15-minute Isochrone)

Clicking any clinic on the map loads a 15-minute drive-time catchment and computes three metrics — population served, competitive density, and nearest rival distance — directly from that polygon. This is distinct from the SA3-level scores above: it characterises the individual clinic's local market, not the broader region.

I
Isochrone construction
15-minute drive time · Mapbox Isochrone API
Each catchment polygon is pre-computed using the Mapbox Isochrone API with a 15-minute driving contour centred on the clinic's geocoded coordinates. Drive-time contours reflect real road network conditions (speed limits, road class) rather than straight-line buffers. All 7,845 isochrones are stored as individual GeoJSON files and fetched on demand — no real-time API call is made when a clinic is selected.
Contour type — drive-time (not distance); 15 minutes from clinic coordinates Source — Mapbox Isochrone API v1, road-network routing Vintage — pre-computed and static; not re-queried in real time
II
Population served
Aggregate SA1 population · ABS Census 2021
The population within a clinic's catchment is estimated by aggregating ABS 2021 Census population counts at the Statistical Area Level 1 (SA1) — the smallest published census geography, typically 200–800 residents. For each SA1 whose centroid falls inside the isochrone polygon, its total person count (Tot_P_P from Census table G01) is added to the catchment total. SA1s whose centroid falls outside the polygon are excluded, even if part of their area overlaps — this is a centroid-in-polygon approximation.
Population served = Σ Tot_P_P for all SA1s where centroid ∈ isochrone
SA1 universe — 61,811 SA1s covering all of Australia (2021 ABS boundaries) SA1 geometry — Centroid coordinates pre-computed and cached as CSV for performance Point-in-polygon test — Turf.js booleanPointInPolygon() for each SA1 centroid against isochrone Feature polygon Census table — G01 Selected Person Characteristics, ABS Census 2021 Field — Tot_P_P (total persons, both sexes) by SA1 Approximation — centroid-in-polygon only; partial SA1 area overlap at catchment boundary is not pro-rated. SA1s straddling the isochrone edge are included or excluded based solely on whether their centroid falls within the polygon. Edge case — SA1s with ambiguous or missing centroid data are excluded from the aggregate
Higher Population served indicates a larger addressable market within 15 minutes of the clinic. Population figures are 2021 Census counts (not forward-projected); fast-growing outer suburban areas may be slightly understated. Interpret alongside Competitive Density to assess market saturation.
III
Competitive density
Clinics and GPs within catchment
All clinics from the Foundry database whose coordinates fall inside the isochrone polygon are counted as competitors. GP headcounts are summed across those clinics to give total GPs in catchment. Density (clinics per km²) is the clinic count divided by the isochrone area in km². The national average benchmark is the count-weighted median of per-SA3 clinic density across all 336 SA3s — weighted by clinic count so that the reference reflects where clinics actually operate rather than averaging across vast, low-density outback SA3s.
Density = clinics in catchment ÷ isochrone area (km²)
Clinic universe — 7,845 GP practices with validated coordinates National avg — count-weighted median of SA3 clinic densities (~0.15 clinics/km²) GPs in catchment — sum of gp_count for all clinics within polygon
IV
Nearest rival distance
Straight-line · Haversine formula via Turf.js
The distance from the selected clinic to the nearest other clinic whose coordinates fall within the isochrone is computed as a straight-line (Haversine) distance using Turf.js. This identifies the most proximate in-catchment competitor, not necessarily the nearest clinic on the road network. A large nearest-rival distance indicates a relatively protected local position; a small distance indicates high proximate competition.
Method — Haversine great-circle distance (Turf.js turf.distance) Scope — in-catchment clinics only; rivals outside the isochrone are excluded Unit — kilometres, rounded to one decimal place
V
Catchment comparison (two-clinic mode)
Polygon intersection · Turf.js
Clicking a second clinic activates comparison mode, rendering both isochrones on the map and computing overlap metrics. The overlap percentage is the intersecting polygon area divided by the first clinic's total catchment area. Net unique catchment is calculated as the union area minus the intersection — showing the combined addressable market if both clinics are considered as part of a single network. Clinic pairs are categorised as: Complementary (<30% overlap) — minimal patient overlap, geographically distinct; Overlapping (30–70%) — significant but not complete market overlap; Highly Overlapping (>70%) — similar catchment footprints, strong cannibalization risk. Head-to-head metrics (population served, residents per clinic, rival count) are computed independently for each catchment using the same SA1 centroid-in-polygon aggregation logic, allowing direct comparison of market characteristics.
Overlap % = area(iso₁ ∩ iso₂) ÷ area(iso₁) × 100
Net Unique Catchment = area(iso₁ ∪ iso₂) − area(iso₁ ∩ iso₂)
Geometry union — turf.union() on both isochrone Features to compute combined area Geometry intersection — turf.intersect() on the two isochrone Feature polygons to compute overlapping area Area calculation — turf.area() returns area in square metres; converted to km² for display Population in overlap zone — SA1s whose centroid falls in the intersection polygon; counted in both clinics' individual population figures but represent shared addressable market Clinic 1 colour — purple #7C3AED (primary selection) Clinic 2 colour — orange #E8862C (secondary selection) Overlap zone — semi-transparent blend where both isochrones intersect
Use overlap metrics to assess portfolio consolidation potential. High overlap suggests opportunity to rationalize clinic footprints or optimize GP scheduling. Low overlap indicates geographic complementarity — combining networks may fill service gaps. View in context of local demand growth and competitive density within each zone.

Data coverage

The clinic universe was sourced from the NHSD National Health Practitioner Directory (Australian health practitioners). Clinic enrichment includes website URLs (Google Places API), GP counts (web scraping + manual enrichment), format classification (heuristics + GP count thresholds), and service availability (Billing Type, Allied Health, Pathology, Imaging).

For analyses dependent on enriched fields, treat results as representative of clinics with available data; clinics with missing data may differ systematically in size, location remoteness, or web presence.

Limitations

The Workforce Risk Score imputes GP workforce metrics from MMM-class national benchmarks rather than SA3-level actuals — HWD does not publish at the SA3 level. All SA3s sharing the same dominant MMM class receive the same FTE/10k and % ≥55 values; local variation within a class is not captured. DPA shapefiles are 2020 vintage and may not reflect reclassifications since that date. The model does not capture practice-level financials, GP retention risk, or local referral dynamics. Use it as a top-down screening tool, then layer bottom-up underwriting on shortlisted regions.
